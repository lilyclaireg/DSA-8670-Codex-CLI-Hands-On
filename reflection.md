# Reflection

## Where Codex Struggled

I actually didn't run into many issues of struggling with my prompting with Codex. 
I believe that this is mainly due to my initial prompt.
I have been learning about prompt engineering over the past couple of months, and a big theme I've taken away is to have thoughtful prompts.
Before my learning of prompt engineering, I was quicker to have shorter, less thought-out prompts.
If you look at my initial prompt, you can see I gave it a role, background context, clear steps of tasks, folder organization, etc.
This allowed Codex to really understand the question at hand and overall project before moving into actual steps and coding. 
If I had instead just jumped into it without thinking through the steps, I could foresee many issues I might've run into, such as confusion about the overall goal.
Slowing down before prompting is very important. Creating high-quality prompts is very important. Good in means good out; bad in means bad out.
What I mean by this is that our prompts are inputs to these AI models; thus, we should have good prompts if we expect good outputs.
Thoughtless prompts often times means thoughtless outputs.

## How Your Prompts Had To Evolve

My prompts did evolve by adding in things I didn't initially think about, thus weren't included in my initial prompt.
As I was working through this assignment, different ideas came to mind. 
For example, I realized the analysis folder was very cluttered; something I didn't foresee.
So, I pivoted and decided to organize that folder before moving on to the steps I had initially created. 
Another example is creating the final report.
I realized there was so much great material produced from the Codex Session, but it was all stored in different files.
In the real world, you wouldn't give an exec 15 different files to showcase your analysis. 
Instead, you would give them a final report.
With this in the back of my mind, I decided to pause and had Codex produce this final report.
As previously stated, I was really impressed with the final report, including its organization, aesthetics, and communication.
Overall, initially laying out our steps is helpful when prompting, as it gives AI clear order and expectations.
However, there will be times when we need to adjust the steps taken. 

## One Thing You Would Do Differently If You Ran The Task Again

On that same note, something I would do differently is add a note in my initial prompt stating that this is the general order of steps;
There may be steps added, deleted, and changed, but this is the general format.
I think this would help with flexibility in the chat. 
Although I didn't face Codex being inflexible, when prompting in the past, I have noticed that changes to the initial plan can throw AI off.
For example, it may get confused on where to pick back up, or how to implement these changes into the final output or plan.
Thus, including adaptability apart of the plan can help avoid this.
